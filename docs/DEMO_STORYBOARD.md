# RADAR film: "The Calibration Laboratory"

Final storyboard and production specification for the 30-second advertisement.
Written 2026-10-08. This replaces the first storyboard (eight scenes of animated type),
which survives only as a timing sketch in `demo-video/`. Nothing here is implemented.

- **Format:** 1920 × 1080, 30 frames a second, 900 frames.
- **One idea:** we asked which way, could not find a reliable answer, and found that
  how far could be measured as a range. The person still decides.
- **Feeling at the end:** clarity and trust. Not triumph.

## 1. What the film may claim

Checked against `docs/DECISIONS.md` and notebooks 02, 03 and 07 before writing.

| The film shows | The record behind it |
|---|---|
| Instruments making a call on direction, and landings that ignore the call | No signal or model tested passed its pre-written test on direction. The record's own wording: "no measurable difference" means "smaller than this data can show", not "none" |
| A plain instrument whose trace follows the spread, roughly | A forecast of how much a market moves had the lowest error of four methods in 6 of 6 comparisons. Not every pairwise gap was beyond luck |
| Drawers of past days, sorted by how rough they were | Each simulated day is drawn from the real past returns seen in the market's current state |
| One outcome built from many small pushes | One simulated path is a chain of drawn days, up to 30 |
| Ten thousand balls forming a shape, some leaving the bed | 10,000 simulated paths. Before adjustment the ranges missed their stated coverage by 3.3 points on average and up to 8.5 |
| One ball in a hand, undecided | The product does not say which way, when to buy, or that a move unlike the past is inside the range |

Tags that appear in shot are diegetic and literal: "no reliable difference found" and,
on the seismograph, "closer than the others".

## 3. The world

One room, about six metres by four, with one tall north window on the left wall.

| Element | Description |
|---|---|
| **Drop tower** | 1.6 m, blackened steel frame with brass fittings, standing at the head of the bed. Top: a release gate and hopper. Middle: the instrument mount. Lower third: a cascade of forty small brass paddles in staggered rows, enclosed in glass, present and visible from the first wide view |
| **Landing bed** | A slate trough 2.4 m long, filled with fine pale sand raked flat. One hairline of brass is inlaid down the centre |
| **Mount** | A dovetail cradle with a locking lever. On its front, a small brass flag on a pivot: it tips left or right |
| **Shelf** | Oiled walnut, the length of the back wall. Six instruments, each with a blank manila tag on waxed thread |
| **Drawer wall** | Three walnut cabinets of small drawers on the right wall. Each cabinet has one engraved plate: CALM, ORDINARY, ROUGH |
| **Technician** | Hands and forearms. Sleeves of undyed linen rolled to the elbow. No watch, no rings, no face |

**The instruments.** Each stands for a family of signals the project tested.

| Instrument | Stands for | Build |
|---|---|---|
| Pendulum gauge (hero) | Readings of "too high" and "too low" | A blued steel needle between two brass stops, under a glass dome |
| Etched glass | Price levels | A thick plate ruled with fine horizontal lines, in a brass frame |
| Rail | Trend | A curved brass channel on an adjustable arm |
| Vane | The crowd | A small weathervane inside a ring of six fans |
| Clockwork | Learned models (the tree models only) | An open movement of wheels that re-sets itself between drops |
| Seismograph | Size of movement | A paper drum, a stylus arm, a lead weight. Plain. Bottom shelf, far end |

## 4. Storyboard

Nine shots. Frame ranges are inclusive.

### Shot 1 · 0:00 to 0:04.0 · frames 0 to 119 · Confidence

- **Purpose:** make the instruments credible and desirable, then reveal the room.
- **Camera:** starts 20 cm from the shelf at instrument height. Slow lateral dolly left to
  right for three seconds, then eases back and up into a wide view of the room.
- **Lens feel:** 90 mm macro to begin, breathing out to 28 mm.
- **Subject:** the pendulum gauge, the etched glass, the rail, the vane, the clockwork,
  passing in shallow focus. The seismograph is last and least lit.
- **Subject movement:** the gauge's needle drifts. The clockwork ticks once.
- **Environment:** dust in the window light. A blank tag turns on its thread.
- **Lighting:** cool, even window light. Soft highlights travel across brass as the
  camera moves.
- **Depth:** foreground instrument sharp, the next two soft, the tower a dark vertical
  shape far behind, resolving as the camera pulls back.
- **Sound:** room tone. One tick. Cloth on wood as a hand reaches into frame at the end.
- **Copy:** "Everyone wants to know which way." Appears at 0:01, gone by 0:03.5.
- **Into next:** the hand lifts the pendulum gauge off the shelf. Cut on the lift.

### Shot 2 · 0:04.0 to 0:08.0 · frames 120 to 239 · The hero test

- **Purpose:** show the whole procedure once, clearly, in a single take.
- **Camera:** close on the mount. Holds as the gauge is seated. Then tracks straight
  down the tower with the falling ball and settles 10 cm above the sand.
- **Lens feel:** 50 mm, then macro at the landing.
- **Subject and movement:** the gauge slides into the dovetail; the lever locks. The
  needle swings to the right-hand stop. The brass flag tips left. The gate opens. One
  steel ball falls past the instrument, through the paddles (which stand open, vertical),
  and lands to the right of the brass line.
- **Environment:** sand lifts in a small crown and settles. A crater remains.
- **Lighting:** unchanged. The ball carries a moving reflection of the window.
- **Depth:** at the landing, the crater sharp in the foreground, the flag still pointing
  left, soft, in the upper background. That frame is the argument.
- **Sound:** dovetail slide, lever click, needle tap, flag tick, gate, a short fall, a
  soft impact in sand.
- **Copy:** "The question was reasonable." Appears at 0:07, after the landing.
- **Into next:** match cut on the crater: the same framing, then the camera lifts.

### Shot 3 · 0:08.0 to 0:11.5 · frames 240 to 344 · The rhythm

- **Purpose:** the remaining tests as one continuous, tightening rhythm.
- **Camera:** one unbroken orbit of about 50 degrees around the tower at bench height,
  slow and constant. The camera's speed never changes; the action inside it does.
- **Lens feel:** 35 mm.
- **Subject and movement:** in stepped time, four instruments pass through the mount:
  glass, rail, vane, clockwork. For each: flag tips, ball falls, crater. The interval
  between drops shortens every time: about 0.9 s, 0.7, 0.5, 0.35, then several at 0.25.
- **Environment:** craters gather on both sides of the brass line. Behind, the shelf
  refills, each instrument now with a pencilled tag.
- **Lighting:** unchanged, and by now a little relentless.
- **Depth:** the orbit keeps tower, bed and shelf in three distinct planes sliding
  against each other.
- **Sound:** the drops become a metronome that speeds up. No music under it.
- **Copy:** none.
- **Into next:** a hard cut to stillness, mid-beat. The expected next drop does not come.

### Shot 4 · 0:11.5 to 0:14.0 · frames 345 to 419 · Silence

- **Purpose:** the stop. Doubt.
- **Camera:** locked off. The only static shot in the film.
- **Lens feel:** 50 mm.
- **Subject:** the empty mount in the foreground, its flag upright. Beyond it, both hands
  resting on the slate edge. Beyond them, the shelf of tagged instruments.
- **Subject movement:** the hands do not move. One tag swings and comes to rest:
  "no reliable difference found".
- **Lighting:** unchanged.
- **Sound:** room tone only, lower than before. One and a half seconds of nothing.
- **Copy:** "I don't know." Appears at 0:13.0.
- **Into next:** the camera begins to move before the cut; the motion carries across.

### Shot 5 · 0:14.0 to 0:16.5 · frames 420 to 494 · The turn

- **Purpose:** the film's hinge. Attention leaves the instruments and finds the evidence.
- **Camera:** from the height of the empty mount, tilts down and cranes forward over the
  bed, finishing directly overhead.
- **Lens feel:** 35 mm. Focus pulls from the shelf, through the hands, to the sand.
- **Subject:** the full crater field, seen whole for the first time.
- **What the viewer discovers:** it is not a mess. Craters crowd near the brass line and
  thin towards the ends of the bed. A few lie very far out.
- **Environment:** the instruments slide out of the top of the frame and are gone.
- **Lighting:** the first change. The window light has dropped slightly, so the craters
  begin to hold shadow.
- **Sound:** room tone, then one low sustained note, barely there.
- **Copy:** none.
- **Into next:** the camera keeps descending; cut into the macro along the same line.

### Shot 6 · 0:16.5 to 0:19.5 · frames 495 to 584 · The second measurement

- **Purpose:** from "which way" to "how far", without claiming more than was found.
- **Camera:** very low, 3 cm above the sand, tracking slowly along the bed.
- **Lens feel:** 100 mm macro, thin focus.
- **Subject and movement:** crater rims in raking light. A hand sets the seismograph at
  the head of the bed. The stylus has drawn a strip as the tower worked. The hand unrolls
  the paper and lays it along the slate edge, beside the craters. Where the trace is
  broad, the craters beside it lie mostly wide; where it is thin, mostly close. In one
  place they do not agree. The tag on the seismograph reads "closer than the others".
- **Lighting:** raking and a little warmer. The relief of the sand is the picture.
- **Depth:** paper strip in the foreground, craters mid, the dark tower base far behind.
- **Sound:** stylus scratch, paper unrolling, paper settling on stone.
- **Copy:** "Not knowing has a shape." Appears at 0:17.2, over the craters, before the
  seismograph arrives.
- **Into next:** the hand leaves frame right, towards the drawers. Cut on the movement.

### Shot 7 · 0:19.5 to 0:25.0 · frames 585 to 749 · Many outcomes

- **Purpose:** the method, made physical; then scale.
- **Camera, in one move:** tracks with the hand to the cabinet marked ORDINARY; follows
  a drawer of steel balls to the hopper; drops into the tower as the gate opens and falls
  with the balls through the paddle cascade; exits at the bottom; then pulls back and
  cranes up to the widest view in the film.
- **Lens feel:** 35 mm in the room, 18 mm inside the tower, 24 mm for the wide.
- **Subject and movement:** the mount swings aside. Before each ball, the forty paddles
  re-set to new angles. A ball strikes them in turn, a small push at each, and leaves the
  cascade somewhere along the bed. Then another, then streams of them. On the bed they
  heap into a long ridge: high in the middle, shallow shoulders, thin tails.
- **What the viewer discovers:** no two take the same way down. The ridge keeps its form
  while every ball in it arrives somewhere different. Six or seven roll past the end of
  the slate and drop to the floor; the camera lets them go.
- **Lighting:** inside the tower, light through glass and between paddles, balls
  flashing as they pass. In the wide, the window light, now warm, grazes the ridge.
- **Depth:** inside the cascade, balls pass within centimetres of the lens while others
  fall far behind. In the wide, the ridge runs away from camera to the window.
- **Sound:** drawer, balls pouring, gate, then steel on brass building from single taps
  to rain, then thinning to single taps again. Two or three balls hit the wooden floor.
- **Copy:** none. No highlight, no arrow, no marked path.
- **Into next:** the last ball settles. The camera descends towards the ridge.

### Shot 8 · 0:25.0 to 0:28.0 · frames 750 to 839 · One ball

- **Purpose:** return to human scale. Acceptance.
- **Camera:** comes down from the wide to bench height, then pushes in to the hand.
- **Lens feel:** 50 mm, ending at 85 mm.
- **Subject and movement:** the hands press two thin brass markers into the sand, either
  side of where the ridge is thick. Balls remain beyond both. One hand lifts a single
  ball from the ridge and holds it in an open palm. The fingers close.
- **Lighting:** warm, low window light. The window is a small bright shape on the ball
  until the fingers cover it.
- **Depth:** palm sharp; ridge and markers soft behind; the tower a shadow.
- **Sound:** two markers into sand. Near silence. Skin closing on steel.
- **Copy:** "We looked for certainty. We found a range." Appears at 0:26.3.
- **Into next:** focus leaves the closed hand and travels to the tower's base.

### Shot 9 · 0:28.0 to 0:30.0 · frames 840 to 899 · The name

- **Purpose:** the brand, without leaving the room.
- **Camera:** finishes the focus pull and holds. A very slight push.
- **Lens feel:** 85 mm.
- **Subject:** a small brass maker's plate on the tower's base, present since shot 1 and
  never before in focus. Engraved: RADAR.
- **Lighting:** the warm light crosses the engraving.
- **Depth:** the closed hand stays in the frame, soft, lower left.
- **Sound:** room tone. One steel ball rolls to rest somewhere out of frame.
- **Copy:** RADAR on the plate. Under it, the super: "It measures. You decide."
- **End:** hold four frames after the last word. Cut to black.

## 5. Materials

| Material | Character |
|---|---|
| Pale sand | Very fine, almost flour, the colour of unbleached paper. Holds a crisp crater rim. Matte, with a faint sparkle only in raking light |
| Steel balls | 6 mm, polished bearing steel. Mirror-bright; each carries the window. Not chrome-blue: neutral |
| Brass | Unlacquered, hand-finished, slightly darkened in the recesses. Satin, never gold-shiny. Fine machining marks on flat faces |
| Glass | Thick, with a green edge. Dust and one fingerprint. Real refraction at the plate's edges |
| Paper | Manila tags, soft fibre, pencil that has slightly smudged. Seismograph strip: thin, slightly translucent, curls at the ends |
| Slate | The bed and bench top. Honed, dark grey, with chalk-pale scuffs |
| Walnut | Shelf and cabinets. Oiled, open grain, worn pale at drawer pulls |
| Blackened steel | The tower frame. Dry, slightly uneven, with brighter worn edges |
| Blued steel | The gauge needle and the stylus arm. A thin dark blue that flashes once |
| Linen | The sleeves. Undyed, visibly woven |
| Skin | Natural, unretouched, a little dry. Clean short nails |

## 6. Lighting

One motivated source: the north window. No practical lamps, no coloured light.

| Shots | Window light | Fill | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 to 4 | High, cool, broad. About 6,200 K | Soft bounce off a pale wall | Even, exact, a little clinical |
| 5 | Begins to lower | Slightly less | Craters start to hold shadow |
| 6 | Low and raking. About 5,000 K | Low | The sand's relief becomes the image |
| 7 | Warm, about 4,200 K, grazing the ridge | Low | Scale and depth |
| 8 to 9 | Warm, about 3,600 K, low | Very low | Intimate. The hand is the brightest thing |

The change is the afternoon passing. It is never a grade applied on top.

**2D elements**

- Five supers, one typeface, one size, lower left
- Pencil lettering for the tags (as textures)
- Engraving for the three cabinet plates and the maker's plate (as textures)
- The seismograph trace (a texture generated from the film's own crater data)

**Cameras:** nine, one per shot, each an authored path. Shots 2, 5 and 7 need focus pulls.

**Lighting elements:** one window area light with animated height, intensity and colour
temperature; one bounce fill; an environment map of a plain interior for reflections in
steel and brass; light dust in the window beam (shots 1 and 4 only).

**Simulation and physics**

| System | Shots | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Single ball drop with sand crown and crater | 2 | Hero quality |
| Repeated drops, accumulating craters | 3 | Same system, lower detail |
| Crater field layout | 3 to 6 | Generated once from a seeded draw so every shot agrees |
| Paddle cascade and ten thousand balls | 7 | Paths computed ahead of time from a seeded draw, then played back |
| Ridge build-up | 7 to 8 | Balls stacking; may be approximated by a growing surface with loose balls on top |
| Balls leaving the bed | 7 | A handful, hand-placed |
| Cloth, paper, thread | 1, 4, 6 | Small secondary motion |

Every simulation must be computed in advance and replayed by frame number. Nothing may
run live.

**Genuine 3D required:** shots 1, 2, 3, 5, 7 and 8. Camera travel, parallax, reflections
and physical contact are the content of these shots.

**Could be 2.5D without visible loss:** shot 4 (locked off; a rendered plate with the tag
and dust as separate layers) and shot 9 (a held macro with a focus pull). Shot 6 could be
a rendered plate with a slow move across it if the paper is a separate layer.

## 9. Planning for Remotion

Remotion is the timeline, the titles, the sound and the final render.

| Composition | Frames | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `Shot1Shelf` | 0 to 119 | |
| `Shot2HeroTest` | 120 to 239 | |
| `Shot3Rhythm` | 240 to 344 | Hard cut out |
| `Shot4Silence` | 345 to 419 | |
| `Shot5Turn` | 420 to 494 | |
| `Shot6SecondMeasurement` | 495 to 584 | |
| `Shot7ManyOutcomes` | 585 to 749 | |
| `Shot8OneBall` | 750 to 839 | |
| `Shot9Name` | 840 to 899 | |
| `Film` | 0 to 899 | The nine in order; cuts only, no dissolves |

- **Reusable assets:** the room, the tower, the bed and the ball are shared by every shot.
- **Reusable systems:** one camera-path system; one window-light system driven by a
  single "time of day" value per shot; one seeded data file holding crater positions and
  the ten thousand paths, read by shots 3 to 8.
- **Typography:** one super component, used five times. Inter, medium weight, about
  44 px, lower left, inside the safe area.
- **Audio:** one track of recorded or licensed foley per shot, plus the sustained note.

**A production decision that has to be made before any implementation.** The Remotion
skills available here cover a Three.js canvas with frame-driven animation. They do not
cover depth of field, soft shadows from an area light, refraction, sand, or
rigged hands, and a real-time renderer will struggle to make this film look tactile and
expensive. There are two routes:

| Route | What it means | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Pre-rendered** | Build and light the room in a 3D package (Blender is connected to this session), bake the physics, render each shot as an image sequence, and assemble, title and score it in Remotion | Looks like the storyboard. Slow renders. Each shot becomes a video layer in Remotion, so its camera is not editable there |
| **B. Real-time** | Build everything in Three.js inside Remotion | Fully editable in Studio. Will not match this specification for sand, focus, glass or hands without heavy compromise |

Route A is the honest recommendation for the standard set here. Route B suits an
animatic first: block all nine shots with simple geometry to prove timing and camera,
then replace shot by shot.

**The hardest asset is the hands.** Convincing bare hands are the largest single risk on
either route. Options, in order of preference: film real hands for shots 4 and 8 and
match the light; use a licensed rigged hand model and keep hands at the frame edge in
soft focus; or put the technician in white cotton gloves, which suits the room and is far
easier to make believable.

No outside asset has been downloaded. Models, foley and any footage need their licences
checked before they enter this public repository.

## 10. Hostile review, and what changed

The first draft had fourteen shots. It was reviewed against fifteen questions; these are
the ones it failed.

| Question | Verdict on the first draft | Change made |
|---|---|---|
| Real advertisement, not a demo? | Fourteen shots in thirty seconds averaged two seconds each: restless, the opposite of quiet and expensive | Cut to nine. The establishing view folded into the end of shot 1; the hero test became one take; drawer, release and crane became one move |
| Does the testing feel cinematic, not repetitive? | Five separate tests were five small scenes | One orbit at constant speed with the drops accelerating inside it, then a hard cut to stillness |
| Is the turn causal? | The cloud of outcomes arrived as a new idea | The paddle cascade is part of the tower from shot 1, and the hero ball falls through it. The drawers are on the wall from shot 1. Nothing new enters the room |
| Does the simulation avoid implying a forecast? | Brass markers placed during the wide shot read as a result being announced | Markers moved to shot 8, at human scale, with balls visibly outside them |
| Is the final moment strong? | Three lines in the last four seconds; the hand had one second | Shot 8 given three seconds, the hand alone for the last of them; the name moved onto an object |
| Too much text? | The seismograph shot carried a line and a tag at once | The line now lands on the craters, before the instrument arrives |
| Any copy explaining the picture? | No | None |
| Generic or template-like? | The end card left the room for a logo on a plain ground | The name is engraved on the tower's maker's plate |
| Recognisable without the logo? | Yes: nobody else tests the instruments and shelves them intact | None |

**What the review could not fix.**

- A ball means one day in shots 2 and 3 and one whole outcome in shot 7. The cascade is
  what joins the two: the test balls fall past open paddles, the later ones are pushed
  by all forty. A viewer may not consciously read this. It is accurate, and it is subtle.
- The seismograph has three seconds. That is enough to see paper laid beside sand and a
  rough agreement. It is not enough to understand a forecast, and the film should not try.
- "ORDINARY" on the cabinet is a plain word for the middle market state. It is legible
  for under a second.
- Thirty seconds is tight for nine shots at this pace. A 45-second cut would let shots 3
  and 7 breathe and is worth considering before production.
